Symmetric Encryption (secret-key ciphering)
    Use a single key for both encryption and decryption
    The same key is used by both the sending and receiving parties
    The key is kept confidential and must be exchanged securely.
    Ideal for quickly encrypting large amounts of data.
    Advanced Encryption Standard (AES)
        https://www.devglan.com/online-tools/aes-encryption-decryption
        Data is encrypted in 128-bit blocks,
        which are then encrypted using 128, 192, and 256-bit keys.
    Others (legacy or obsolete)
        Data Encryption Standard (DES)
        3DES
        Rivest Cipher 4 (RC4)
    There are many use cases for symmetric encryption, including:
        WiFi: WPA2 uses symmetric encryption to secure communication between a device and a router.
        Disk encryption: Encrypts all data, including temporary files and swap space. 
        Data encryption: Transparent Data Encryption (TDE) in SQL servers uses AES to protect data at rest

Asymmetric Encryption (public-key encryption)
    Uses a pair of public and private keys
    The public key is available to anyone, while the private key remains confidential.
    offers a solution to the key distribution problem faced by symmetric digital security.
    enables the creation of digital signatures, providing authenticity
    Rivest-Shamir-Adleman (RSA)
        Security is based on the difficulty of factoring large integers composed of two or more large prime numbers.
        https://www.devglan.com/online-tools/rsa-encryption-decryption
    Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC)
    Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA)
    Here are some of the most common ways asymmetric encryption is used:
        Secure Data Transmission: Used in SSL/TLS for secure web browsing.
            Encrypted HTTPS communication relies in part on asymmetric key pairs.
        Digital Signatures: Ensures the authenticity and integrity of a message.
        Key Exchange: Used to securely exchange symmetric keys for encryption algorithms like AES.
        Email encryption: Most email providers use TLS to encrypt messages and thus rely on public key encryption methods.

    https://www.practicalnetworking.net/series/cryptography/using-asymmetric-keys/

Real World Encryption
    Use asymmetric encryption to share a symmetric key

Real World Signatures
    Run the message through a Hashing Algorithm, then encrypt the resulting digest with Private Key.
    Send the encrypted digest along with the original message.
    Use public key to decrypt the received digest, then calculate independently the hash of the original message.
    Compare the (now decrypted) digest which was sent, and the digest which he calculated.

Red Team are the breakers or the ethical hackers,
    their role is to act as Attackers and search for security holes that a
    malicious individual can exploit.
Blue Team are the Defenders.
    Responsible from protective measure within an organization.
Purple Team's are the Defenders.
    Responsible from protective measure within an organization.
Yellow Team are the people that build and design software, systems, and
    integrations that make business more efficient. Their focus is usually on
    requirements, functionality, user experience and back-end performance.
Orange Team's main objective is to inspire Yellow Team to be more security
    conscious, increasing their security awareness by providing education to
    benefit software code and design implementation.
    There should be structured and ongoing engagements between Red and Yellow
    Team for the benefit of Yellow. You want your Orange Team to teach your
    developers to think like attackers.
Green Team consists of ongoing structured interactions between the Blue Team
    and members of the software team (Yellow Team). The ultimate goal is to
    improve code-based and design-based defense capability for detection,
    incident response and data forensics.
White Team members include elements of Compliance, Management, Analysts,
    Logistics and more. These are all-knowing, neutral, third-party individuals
    who set the rules of engagement, organize teams, set strategy, perform risk
    assessments and sets plans and monitors progress. They’re essential for
    every organisation, but they’re seen as “too corporate” for the developers,
    “too demanding” for the defenders and “too much of a killjoy” for the attackers.
